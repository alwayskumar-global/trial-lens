## 2. Why criteria stay text (from the cache, and from hand-built atoms run through the real checks)
The cache stores vetted outcomes only, not the parser's raw output, so "the parser emitted text" and "vetting downgraded it" can be separated only by vet status. Findings:
1. **Parser emits text for plainly typeable single propositions** (vet `ok`, text leaf): "Confirmed TNBC (ER-, PR-, HER2-)", "Stage IV (Metastatic) breast cancer", "Metastatic disease (Stage IV)", "History of radiotherapy", "Recurrent or metastatic breast cancer", "Heart ... LVEF ≥50%". Prompt-side issue; needs a model re-parse to change.
2. **Numeric ranges and lists are rejected by the scope check.** Hand-built atom `ecog lte 1` for "ECOG score 0-1 points" → `unaccounted_number`, `unaccounted_qualifier`; "ECOG 0-2" the same; "stage I, II, or III" needs `in`. All 9 untyped ECOG criteria are vetting failures (`atoms_downgraded` ×7, `coverage_failed` ×2); the original parser atoms are not stored, so the range rule is the likely cause, not a proven one (this is why step 2 of section 4 stores raw output). 4 stage-list criteria exist in the cohort.
3. **Unit conversion is rejected.** "ANC ≥ 1,000/mm^3" and "Platelet ≥ 100,000/mm^3" with the vocabulary unit 10^9/L → `unit_not_in_source`, `unaccounted_number`. "Hemoglobin ≥ 90 g/L" vs vocabulary g/dL is the same class.
4. **Cue gaps are not the problem for ECOG**: the ecog cue already matches "Eastern Cooperative"; those failures are scope. "Evaluable lesion" is correctly refused as measurable disease.
5. **Cue noise (soundness risk, not a coverage gain)**: `days_since_last_systemic_therapy` cue matches 154 of the 472, `prior_other_malignancy` 111 (matches the word "cancer"), `disease_setting` 79. These cues cannot catch a wrong-key atom for those keys; they only cost nothing today because few such atoms are emitted. Any change must not widen them.
6. **No vocabulary key for "has breast cancer / histologically confirmed"**, which is a core `diagnosis` criterion in at least 6 of the 8 near-liftable trials. It can never be typed with the current vocabulary.
7. Compound criteria ("neoadjuvant or adjuvant ... and ...", exceptions "except ...") fail coverage by design; that is the intended soundness behaviour.

Measures to report on EVERY cohort, strict and unmodified: (1) **strict typed %** of scoring criteria (the gate measure, never relaxed); (2) core-blocked distribution and the number of UNCERTAIN trials within 1 and 2 repairs (leading indicator); (3) typed-counterfactual lifts on the three prepared profiles (a prediction); (4) **actual lifts after the full re-evaluation** on those profiles (the only number that supports a scope decision); (5) share of runs with at least one question after the zero-gain filter; (6) **false-typed rate** (typed atoms whose reviewed meaning differs from the criterion) on a reviewed sample, plus the existing adversarial regression suites (`coverage.test.ts`, `blocks.test.ts`) as a hard no-regression guard. A coverage gain with a higher false-typed rate is a failure.

**Expected effect (an estimate from this audit, not a measurement).** L1+L2 alone address about 20 of the 488 scoring criteria (9 ECOG + 4 stage lists + 7 unit labs, about +4 points; fewer if the criterion has other unaccounted words); P1 could add part of the ~45 single propositions (up to about +9 points). So strict typed coverage would most likely end around **8-15%, still far below the 40% STOP line**. The realistic goal of Option C is therefore not passing the gate: it is moving trials at distance 1-2 (8 trials in this cohort) to liftable and seeing whether any real lift appears for a prepared profile. If the real lift stays 0, the scope decision returns to Kumar (Option A or B). Option C cannot by itself make the gate pass.

## 5. Preventing overconfident `known` facts (proposal from the recorded failures; no calls, no tuning)
`eval/overconfidence-offline.ts` applies simple deterministic guards to the recorded outputs of hardened-1 and hardened-2 (key names only) and the fictional texts. Recorded wrong-`known` facts per arm: 11 (5 non-forged + 6 forged).
- **G1: a `known` fact needs a cue word for its key somewhere in the text** (the existing `CUES` list). Catches the inference errors: `prior_chemo_any` inferred from "taxane"/"anthracycline" (2 cases) and `cns_mets` inferred in `plain-metastatic-her2`. Downgrade to `unknown`.
- **G2: if the sentence holding the key's cue contains a hedge** ("I think", "I believe", "maybe", "not sure", "I don't know", "as far as I know", "that I know of", "about", "approximately"), downgrade `known` to `uncertain`. Catches the recorded hedged-known facts: `cardiac_disease` (2 cases), `er_status` in `plain-hedged`.
- **Correction (2026-10-07).** The paragraph below first reported the cost as 1 of 57/64 because it excluded G1 from the collateral count, which was wrong: G1 as first written (cue required for EVERY key) would have wrongly dropped **17 of 64** correctly-known gold facts (age stated as a bare number, sex stated by pronoun, `er/pr/her2` stated as "triple-negative"). The prototype `src/lib/pipeline/overconfidence.ts` therefore exempts `age`/`sex` and accepts "triple-negative"/TNBC as a receptor cue; with that, the numbers below hold (1 of 64 wrongly lowered; 5 of 5 non-forged caught), but the exemptions were added after seeing the collateral, so this is a fit to 12 development cases, not a validation.
- Result on the recorded data: **all 5 non-forged wrong-known facts are caught in both arms** (hardened-1: cardiac ×2, prior_chemo ×2, er; hardened-2: cns_mets, prior_chemo ×2, cardiac, er). Cost: **1 of 57 (hardened-1) / 1 of 64 (hardened-2) correctly known facts would be downgraded** (`disease_setting` in `plain-hedged`, "early stage, maybe stage II"). The offline test credits nothing for the forged facts: G1 "caught" `er_status`, `pr_status`, `cns_mets`, `prior_adc` there only because the forged text writes the key names with underscores (a lexical artifact), so **the six forged facts are still unresolved failures**. G3 (authority/record sentence) did not fire on them either.
- Caveats: n = 12 author-written development cases and guards written after seeing the failures (so the 5/5 is a fit, not a validation); sentence splitting and the cue list are crude; a hedge guard can wrongly downgrade a stated fact ("about 60" when the visitor means 60). G1 only costs recall on facts stated without a cue word.
- Recommended next step (no extraction calls): implement G1+G2 as a tested pure post-extraction function in `profileFromExtraction`, with the recorded failures as regression cases and new hand-written negative cases (stated facts that must stay `known`). It changes extraction output only toward less confidence and cannot add a fact. Needs Kumar's approval; G1 and G2 would be validated on fresh fictional cases at the next approved extraction run, not before.
- For forged facts the options are product decisions, not prompt tuning: keep R2 (every fact is visitor-reported and tier-capped, already enforced) and say so in the visitor review UI; and consider showing the extracted facts for confirmation (Stage 3, paused) so the visitor sees what was read. Nothing here can prove a fact came from a clinic.
